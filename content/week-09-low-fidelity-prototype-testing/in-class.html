Bring your breadboarded project, connected to your React interface, and your HMI document.

Exercise 1: Show It Working (30 min)

In groups of three, each person demonstrates their hero experience end to end on the breadboard: device to screen and screen to device. The others list anything that is not yet working or is still faked. That list is your build plan for the next two hours.

Exercise 2: Give It a Body (60 min)

  1. Fix the breadboard and Arduino into a rough body: foam core, cardboard, an existing box.
  2. Move each input and output to roughly where it will be on the final product. Extend wires with stranded wire where needed.
  3. Draw icons and labels on with a marker.
  4. Shake it gently. If anything comes loose, fix it now.

Exercise 3: Write Your Round 1 Test (30 min)

  1. Take your Must-have interactions from the HMI document. Write each one as a task with no method in it.
  2. Break each task into steps, and note an expected time.
  3. Set up Tables A and B (one row per step in Table A).
  4. Decide who your naive participant is and when you will test with them.

Exercise 4: Pilot (20 min)

Run one task with a classmate, exactly as you will in the real test: one-sentence introduction, task, silence, score. Fix your task wording and your start-up routine based on what went wrong. A pilot is not counted as one of your participants.
