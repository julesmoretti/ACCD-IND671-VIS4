Bring your breadboarded project connected to your React interface, your HMI document, your marked-up diagram and the rough body you prepared (Week 8, Part 3).

Materials Check (5 min)

Who has foam core, cardboard or a box for Exercise 2? If you need foam core, tell me now: I can buy some during the break.

Review: HMI Document v1 (10 min)

Swap HMI documents in pairs. Check each other's against "Writing good rows" in the Week 8 reading: specific feedback, disconnected and error rows, not everything a Must have. Today's test tasks come from these rows, so fix gaps now.

Exercise 1: Show It Working (30 min)

In pairs, each person demonstrates their hero experience end to end on the breadboard: device to screen and screen to device. Your partner lists anything that is not yet working or is still faked. That list is your build plan for the next two hours.

Exercise 2: Give It a Body (60 min)

  1. Fix the breadboard and Arduino into a rough body: foam core, cardboard, an existing box.
  2. Move each input and output to where your marked-up diagram puts it. Extend with long jumper wires where needed; no soldering.
  3. Draw icons and labels on with a marker.
  4. Shake it gently. If anything comes loose, fix it now.

Break (10 min)

Exercise 3: Write Your Round 1 Test (30 min)

  1. From your HMI document, pick 3–5 tasks covering every Must-have in your hero experience. Write each one as a task with no method in it.
  2. Break each task into steps, and note an expected time.
  3. Set up Tables A and B as new tabs in your HMI document, "R1 Table A" and "R1 Table B" (one row per step in Table A).
  4. Decide who your naive participant is and when you will test with them.

Exercise 4: Pilot (20 min)

Run one task with a classmate, exactly as you will in the real test: one-sentence introduction, task, silence, score. Fix your task wording and your start-up routine based on what went wrong. A pilot is not counted as one of your participants.
