Introduction

This week your prototype moves off the cardboard and into its intended form. Round 2 tests whether the interactions still work on the real geometry: reach, grip, position, sequence. It is still visibly a print: no paint or finish yet, some tape is fine.

1. Designing an Enclosure Around Electronics

  • Clearance: FDM prints are not exact. Allow about 0.2–0.5 mm per side where a part must slide into a printed pocket. Print a small fit test before the full shell.
  • Mounting the Arduino: the UNO has 3.2 mm mounting holes for M3 screws. Print standoffs into the base rather than gluing the board flat, so nothing on its underside shorts against anything.
  • Access: keep the USB-C port reachable. You will reprogram the board many times before Week 14.
  • Buttons: a small tactile switch behind a printed cap gives you the shape and feel you designed. Leave the cap a little loose so it does not jam.
  • Light: an LED behind a thin wall (1 mm or less) of white or clear print diffuses into a soft glow. A hole gives a sharp point.
  • Split lines: design the shell in two or more parts that close with screws, so you can open it to fix wiring.

2. Moving Off the Breadboard

  • A full-size breadboard rarely fits inside a product. Options, from quickest to most robust: a mini breadboard, a solderable breadboard (same layout, solder instead of push-in), or perfboard.
  • Move one part at a time and test after each move. If it breaks, you know which change broke it.
  • Use stranded wire for anything going to a part in a lid, a handle or anything that moves. Add strain relief (a cable tie or a printed clip) so a tug pulls on the housing, not on the solder joint.
  • Heat-shrink every exposed joint. Inside a closed shell, an exposed joint touching another one is a short circuit.

3. Printing Without Losing a Week

  • Print the parts that carry interactions first (the panel with the controls), then the rest of the shell.
  • Orientation matters: a surface that a user touches looks best printed facing up or vertical, not on supports.
  • Check print times before you commit. If the full shell takes longer than you have, split it or reduce it to the surfaces that matter for round 2.
