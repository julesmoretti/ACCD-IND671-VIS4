Part 2: Hardware Overview

Next week you write the HMI document for your product. It has to account for every piece of hardware the user touches, sees or hears. This overview is where you list that hardware and what each part means for the user, so nothing is missing when you start.

Start from your midterm "Ready for hardware" list and the notes you took on your parts in Week 6. Fill in one row per part your hero experience needs:

Part Input or output? What it does for the user States or values it has Message to or from the screen Arduino pins What the HMI document must cover
Rotary encoder Input Sets the target number of turns Value 0–100; pressed / not pressed Sends {"target": 40} 2 digital + 1 for the push How the user knows the target changed, on the device and on screen; what pressing does
12-LED ring Output Shows progress toward the target Off, filling, full, blinking for error Receives PROGRESS:25 1 digital What each light pattern means; how "done" differs from "error"

Then draw a simple block diagram, by hand or digitally: the Arduino in the middle, each part around it, a line for each connection. Mark each line B (breadboard for now) or S (will be soldered).

Check your pin count against the UNO R4: 14 digital pins and 6 analog inputs. If you need more than that, flag it now.

Finally, look down the last column. Any part with nothing in it is either missing from your design or not needed. Decide which.

Part 3: Breadboard Your Project's Parts

Get each input and output in your overview working on its own on the breadboard, one at a time:

  • One small sketch per part, printing what it reads (inputs) or proving it responds (outputs) in the Serial Monitor.
  • Start with the parts your hero experience cannot work without. If you run out of time, the rest can follow next week.
  • Note anything that surprised you: a sensor that is noisier than expected, a motor that needs more power than the board gives. These change your design, and it is better to find them now than in Week 10.

You do not need to combine them yet. That is Week 8.

Part 4: Your Library Part, Working

Get the part you claimed in Week 6 working with your UNO R4 on the breadboard. Write the sketch with clear comments: what it does, which pins, and anything a classmate would need to know. Next week you solder it onto perfboard, document it and add it to the library.
