Part 1: Finish the Number Adder Connection

If you did not finish in-class Exercise 5, finish it now: a physical button adds 1 to your React Number Adder, and the on-screen buttons change the LED matrix. Record a short video of both directions working.

Part 2: Breadboard Your Project's Parts

Take your Week 6 anatomy map. Get each input and output working on its own on the breadboard, one at a time:

  • One small sketch per part, printing what it reads (inputs) or proving it responds (outputs) in the Serial Monitor.
  • Start with the parts your hero experience cannot work without.
  • Note anything that surprised you: a sensor that is noisier than expected, a motor that needs more power than the board gives. These change your design, and it is better to find them now than in Week 10.

You do not need to combine them yet. That is Week 8.

Part 3: Your Library Part, Working

Get the part you claimed in Week 6 working with your UNO R4 on the breadboard. Write the sketch with clear comments: what it does, which pins, and anything a classmate would need to know. Next week you document it and add it to the library.

Part 4: React Step 3 of 3, Connect

Add react-use-websocket to your project interface, the same way as in Exercise 5. For this week, show the connection status on screen and log every incoming message to the console. Next week you replace your faked device behaviour with real messages.

  • Video of the Number Adder working in both directions.
  • One sketch per project part in week 7/Hardware, plus a photo of each on the breadboard.
  • Your library part's working sketch and a photo of the circuit.
  • Your project interface showing an open connection, in week 7/Project.
