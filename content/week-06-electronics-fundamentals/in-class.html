Today we meet in the shop. Safety glasses on before any iron is switched on. Irons go back in the stand every time.

By the end of class you will have taken one circuit through the whole path you will use for your project: breadboard it, test it, solder it onto perfboard, test it again.

Exercise 1: Breadboard and Multimeter (30 min)

  1. Wire the UNO R4's 5V to the breadboard's + rail and GND to the − rail. Power the board over USB.
  2. Build: + rail → 1 kΩ resistor → LED long leg; LED short leg → − rail. The LED should light.
  3. With the multimeter on DC volts, measure:
    • between the two rails (expect ~5V),
    • across the resistor,
    • across the LED.
    The last two should add up to the first. Write the numbers down.
  4. Unplug USB. Switch to continuity. Check that one rail is connected along its full length. If it does not beep halfway, your board has a split rail.
  5. Use Ohm's law and your resistor measurement to work out the current through the LED. It should be under 8 mA.

Exercise 2: Demo, Then Strip and Tin (25 min)

Watch the demo first: stripping solid and stranded wire, tinning the iron, tinning a wire, a good joint versus a cold joint.

  1. Strip 5–7 mm from one piece of solid and one piece of stranded wire. Check no strands are cut.
  2. Twist the stranded end tight.
  3. Tin the iron, then tin both wire ends. A tinned end is shiny and evenly coated, not blobbed.
  4. Push both into the breadboard. Notice the difference: the tinned stranded wire now behaves like solid.

Exercise 3: Splice Two Wires (20 min)

  1. Strip and tin the ends of two stranded wires.
  2. Slide a piece of heat-shrink onto one wire first, well away from the joint.
  3. Hold the ends side by side (or twisted together), heat, and add a little solder until it flows through.
  4. Let it cool, slide the heat-shrink over, and shrink it.
  5. Test with continuity. Tug it gently: it should not move.

Exercise 4: Demo, Breadboard to Perfboard (15 min)

Watch the whole path once on a single circuit: the working breadboard version, the layout planned on paper, parts placed through the perfboard, legs bent and soldered, tracks made with solder bridges or bare wire, extra legs trimmed, power wires added, and the finished board tested on the same 5V and GND.

Exercise 5: Your Circuit on Perfboard (45 min)

  1. Take the LED and resistor circuit from Exercise 1. Before you remove it from the breadboard, draw the layout: which perfboard holes each leg goes into, and how the joints connect. Keep parts close together.
  2. Place the resistor and the LED through the perfboard. Bend the legs slightly outward on the back so the parts do not fall out.
  3. Solder each leg. Make the connections between them with the legs themselves or a short piece of bare wire.
  4. Solder a red stranded wire for 5V and a black one for GND. Trim the extra legs, then check with continuity that nothing is bridged that should not be.
  5. Connect the wires back to 5V and GND. It should light exactly as it did on the breadboard.
  6. Trade with a neighbour and inspect each other's joints: shiny volcano, or cold and lumpy?

This is your homework in miniature. At home you do it again with a button added, then document it in Fritzing.

Exercise 6: Look at Your Project Parts (10 min)

Open the parts you brought. For each one, note how it connects: pins that fit a breadboard, loose header pins that need soldering, bare wires, or a connector. Keep the notes. Next week's hardware overview starts from them.
