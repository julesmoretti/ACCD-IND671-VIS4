Today we meet in the shop. Safety glasses on before any iron is switched on, and they stay on until the irons are off.

By the end of class you will have taken one circuit through the whole path you will use for your project: breadboard it, test it, solder it onto perfboard, test it again.

Part 1: Together (about 1 hour)

1. Safety (10 min)

  • Why the glasses: overheated parts can burst (a capacitor held too long under the iron, or wired backwards), clipped leads fly, solder spits.
  • The iron is 300–400°C. It lives in its stand. Never catch a falling iron.
  • The fume extractor: how it works, and where to put it (a hand-width from the joint, smoke pulled away from your face).
  • Wash your hands after soldering; no food or drink at the bench.

2. How a Breadboard Works (10 min)

  • Inside a breadboard: the metal clips under each row of five holes, the long strips under the power rails, the centre gap.
  • Powering it: the power supply module from the BOJACK kit clips onto one end of the breadboard and feeds both rails. Set its jumpers to 5V, power it through its barrel jack, and press its switch. No Arduino today; that is Week 7.

3. Materials (15 min)

  • Wire: solid core (breadboards, wires that never move) versus stranded (anything that moves, bends or vibrates). Gauge: 22 AWG for signals, 18–20 AWG for motors and power.
  • Solder: what the shop supplies, rosin core, diameter.
  • Flux: what it does, flux pen for rework, cleaning residue with isopropyl alcohol and a brush.
  • Heat-shrink: sizing, slide it on before you solder, shrink with the heat gun.

4. The Iron: Tips and Temperature (10 min)

  • The shop station: tip, stand, brass wool, temperature dial.
  • A portable iron (TS80): runs from a USB-C power bank, digital temperature setting, swappable tips.
  • Fine conical tip versus chisel tip: use the biggest tip that fits the joint.
  • Temperature: cooler for small, heat-sensitive parts; hotter for thick wire and big pads. Aim for a joint in 2–3 seconds.

5. Demo: Breadboard to Perfboard (15 min)

One circuit, the whole path: the working breadboard version, the layout planned on paper, parts placed through the perfboard, legs bent and soldered, connections made with legs or bare wire, extra legs trimmed, power wires added, a continuity check, then the finished board tested on the same 5V and GND.

Part 2: Two Rotations (2 × 45 min, with a short break between)

There are three soldering stations. Group A (three of you) starts at the irons while Group B (two of you) works on breadboards. Then you swap.

At the soldering station

  1. Strip and tin (10 min): strip 5–7 mm from one solid and one stranded wire, twist the stranded end, tin the iron, tin both ends. A tinned end is shiny and evenly coated, not blobbed.
  2. Splice two wires (15 min): heat-shrink on first, join the tinned ends, let it cool, shrink it, check continuity, tug gently.
  3. LED and resistor on perfboard (20 min): draw the layout first, place a 1 kΩ resistor and an LED, solder, add a red stranded wire for 5V and a black one for GND, trim the legs, check continuity, then connect it to the power module's rails. It should light.

At the breadboard bench

  1. Breadboard and multimeter (25 min): clip the power supply module onto the breadboard, set both jumpers to 5V, and switch it on. Build + rail → 1 kΩ resistor → LED long leg; LED short leg → − rail. Measure the voltage between the rails, across the resistor and across the LED; the last two should add up to the first. Switch the module off, set the meter to continuity, and check one rail along its full length.
  2. Add a button (10 min): put a push button between the + rail and the resistor. The LED should light only while it is pressed. This is your homework circuit; draw its perfboard layout now.
  3. Fritzing (10 min): open Fritzing and start drawing the button circuit in the Breadboard view: breadboard, power source, button, resistor, LED.

Part 3: Wrap-Up (15 min)

  • Inspect each other's joints: shiny volcano, or cold and lumpy? Fix one cold joint together.
  • Your project parts: for each one, note how it connects (pins that fit a breadboard, loose header pins that need soldering, bare wires, or a connector). Keep the notes. Next week's hardware overview starts from them.
  • Homework: take a perfboard and lengths of red and black stranded wire before you leave. You will solder the button circuit in the shop during the week.
