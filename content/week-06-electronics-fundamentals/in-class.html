We start in the classroom, then move to the shop for soldering. Bring your safety glasses and your BOJACK kit.

Today you take one small circuit through the whole path you will use for your project: breadboard it, test it, solder it, test it again.

The Class Circuit

Two LEDs, one button and the power module's switch, all from your BOJACK kit:

  • Switch the module on: the red LED lights. The circuit has power.
  • Press the button: the green LED lights while you hold it.
  • Switch the module off: everything goes dark.

Parts: the power supply module (both jumpers on 5V), 2 × 1 kΩ resistors, 1 red LED, 1 green LED, 1 push button, jumper wires.

LEDs only work one way round. The long leg (+) goes toward the positive side of the circuit, the short leg (−) toward the negative side. The rim of the LED is also flat on the short-leg side. A backwards LED simply stays dark: turn it round.

Part 1: Watch Me (classroom, about 45 min)

1. How a Breadboard Works (10 min)

  • What is under the plastic: the metal clips under each row of five holes, the long strips under the power rails, the centre gap.
  • The power supply module: where it plugs in, the 5V jumpers, the switch, which rail is + and which is −.
  • Safe on the breadboard: switch off before you rewire; never + straight to −; every LED gets a resistor.

2. Demo: Build the Class Circuit (15 min)

I build it in front of you, one part at a time, switching on after each step: red LED first, then the button branch. Watch where each leg goes.

3. Demo: The Multimeter (10 min)

  • The leads: black always goes in COM. Red goes in VΩmA for everything we do today: volts, resistance, continuity. The 10A socket is only for measuring large currents, which we do not do in this class; a red lead left there turns the meter into a short circuit the moment you touch it across a supply.
  • Choose the mode and range before the probes touch anything. Our meters (AstroAI AM33D) are manual-ranging: pick the range just above what you expect. For 5V, turn the dial to 20 in the DC volts (V⎓) section.
  • DC volts: between the rails (~5V), across R1, across LED1. The last two add up to the first.
  • Continuity, with the module off: along a rail, across the button (beeps only while pressed).
  • What not to do: mains, measuring with the red lead in 10A, and changing the dial while the probes are on the circuit.

4. Materials (10 min)

  • Wire: solid core versus stranded, and when to use each. Gauge: 22 AWG for signals, 18–20 AWG for motors and power.
  • Solder: what the shop supplies, rosin core, diameter.
  • Flux: what it does, the flux pen, cleaning residue with isopropyl alcohol and a brush.
  • Heat-shrink: sizing, slide it on before you solder, shrink with the heat gun.
  • The solderable breadboard: the same layout as the breadboard, so the circuit moves across hole for hole.

Part 2: Build It Yourself (classroom, 30 min)

  1. Build the class circuit on your breadboard from the table above, long LED leg toward +. Switch on after each branch.
  2. Measure: the voltage between the rails, across R1 and across LED1. Write the three numbers down.
  3. Switch off. With continuity, check the button: it should beep only while pressed.
  4. Take a photo from straight above. You copy it onto the solderable board in the shop.

Part 3: Watch Me Solder (shop, 20 min)

Everyone at one bench, safety glasses on.

  • Safe at the iron: why the glasses (overheated parts can burst, clipped leads fly, solder spits); the stand; never catch a falling iron; no power to the circuit while soldering.
  • The fume extractor: switch it on, a hand-width from the joint, head to one side of the smoke.
  • Two irons: the shop station (stand, brass wool, temperature dial) and a portable TS80 (USB-C power bank, digital temperature, swappable tips). Fine tip versus chisel tip; cooler for small parts, hotter for thick wire; a joint in 2–3 seconds.
  • Demo: tin the tip, then move the class circuit onto a solderable breadboard: parts in the same holes, legs bent, soldered, trimmed; jumpers replaced by short solid wires; red and black stranded leads with heat-shrink; a continuity check; then power it from the module. It should behave exactly as the breadboard did.

Part 4: Solder It Yourself (shop, 2 × 35 min)

Three soldering stations: Group A (three of you) solders first while Group B (two of you) works on Fritzing; then swap.

At the iron

  1. Power leads (10 min): strip 5–7 mm from a red and a black stranded wire, twist, tin the ends. Slide heat-shrink on before you solder them to the board.
  2. The class circuit (25 min): place each part in the same holes as your breadboard photo, solder, trim. Short solid wires for the jumpers, including the rail bridge. Solder the red lead to the top + rail and the black lead to the top − rail at row 1, then shrink the heat-shrink.
  3. Check continuity (nothing bridged that should not be), then plug the leads into your powered breadboard's rails. Red LED on; press, green LED on.

At the Fritzing bench

  1. Open Fritzing and draw the class circuit in the Breadboard view: breadboard, power source, resistors, LEDs, button, wires. Same colours as your real circuit.
  2. Check the Schematic view and tidy it. You finish this for homework.

Wrap-Up (10 min)

  • Inspect each other's joints: shiny volcano, or cold and lumpy? Fix one cold joint together.
  • Your project parts: for each one, note how it connects (pins that fit a breadboard, loose header pins that need soldering, bare wires, or a connector). Next week's hardware overview starts from these notes.
  • If your soldered board is not finished, book shop time this week to finish it.
