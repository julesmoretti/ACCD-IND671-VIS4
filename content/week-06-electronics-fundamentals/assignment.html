Part 1: Solder a Working Circuit and Document It in Fritzing

Take one circuit through the whole path you practised in class: breadboard, test, perfboard, test again, document.

The circuit: a button that lights an LED, the one you breadboarded in class. It needs no code; the Arduino only supplies power.

Soldering happens in the shop, not at home: book a station during the week. You do not need your own iron.

5V → push button → 1 kΩ resistor → LED long leg
LED short leg → GND
  1. Breadboard it and check that the LED lights only while the button is pressed.
  2. Plan the perfboard layout on paper (see the reading, section 8). Take a photo of the plan.
  3. Solder it in the shop onto the perfboard you took from class, with a red stranded wire for 5V and a black one for GND. Safety glasses on, fume extractor on. Check with continuity before you connect power.
  4. Test it on the UNO R4's 5V and GND. It should behave exactly as the breadboard version did.
  5. Document it in Fritzing (reading, section 9; Fritzing is a paid download, about $12): the Breadboard view with the UNO R4 WiFi part from the class library, a tidy Schematic view, the .fzz file and a .jpg export.

If your project already needs a simple circuit that works without code (a power switch, an indicator LED), you can solder that instead. Check it with me on Slack first.

Part 2: Claim a Part for the Class Library

The class library at ACCD-Fritzing-Parts has working examples for: LED, built-in LED, LED matrix, 8x8 LED matrix, 12-LED ring, push button, potentiometer. Over the next two weeks you add one more, taking it through the same path as Part 1.

  1. Pick a component that is not in the library yet. Choose one from your BOJACK kit or your project parts, so there is no shipping wait. One you need for your project is the best choice.
  2. Claim it in the Slack thread for this assignment. First come, first served; no duplicates.
  3. Find its datasheet or product page, and note its voltage and pins.

Week 7: you get it working on your Arduino, on the breadboard. Week 8: you solder it onto perfboard, document it in Fritzing, and add it to the library. The full steps are in the library's CONTRIBUTE.md.
