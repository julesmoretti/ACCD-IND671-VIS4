As discussed during class, this week's assignment is to create an HMI document for your class final presentation.

IMPORTANT NOTE: This document is the start of the HMI design and documentation score of your final project (30 of 100 points), so please make sure that you carefully go through all the points below and that this documentation is very thorough and clear. This will act as a solid foundation for your presentation.

The HMI document should have the following requirements.

  1. A Product Information tab with the following information within it:
    1. Product Name
    2. Description of the product - Intended use, location of usage, and basic functionalities.
    3. Any link to relevant presentation or online portfolio piece of it.
    4. A diagram of the product that includes leader lines to some of the important HMI features
    5. A screenshot of your software interface with leader lines to its controls and the information it shows, placed next to the product diagram
  2. A second tab called UX, with the following:
    1. Interactions (Ex: Power ON)
    2. User Needs (Ex: As a user, when I [perform an action], I expect [desired outcome], so that [benefit or purpose])
    3. Interaction Types (physical button press, touchscreen, voice command)
    4. Feedback (e.g., visual, auditory, haptic)
    5. Priority (How important this feature is for the product functionality)
    6. Prototype Complexity (this is a new column that is similar to the Priority column but that is specifically around how simple or complex this would be to prototype considering what we have covered during class or not)
    7. Software integrations
    8. Notes
    9. And Please separate your interactions by categories for example (Power, Connectivity, Audio Controls, Graphics etc...)

Part 2: Your Hero Experience, End to End on the Breadboard

Combine the parts you breadboarded in Week 7 into one sketch, and connect it to your React project interface through the WebSocket server. The goal: your hero experience runs from start to finish, with the hardware behaving the way the real product will.

  • Work through your hero experience rows from class (Exercise 1). Each row that involves the device should now happen on the device: a real button press, a real light, a real motor.
  • Both directions: inputs on the device change the screen, and controls on the screen change the device.
  • Replace your fakeDevice() behaviour with real messages for every row of the hero experience. Anything outside the hero experience can stay faked.
  • It can be messy. Wires everywhere, parts taped to the desk, all fine. What matters is that it behaves like the product. This is the core of next week's low-fidelity prototype.

If a part has not arrived, fake that one part on the Arduino side (for example, an LED standing in for a motor) and note it.

Part 3: Get Ready for the Low-Fidelity Prototype

Next week your breadboard goes into a rough body that someone else can hold. Prepare for it:

  • Bring a rough body to class: foam core, cardboard or an existing box, roughly the size of your product.
