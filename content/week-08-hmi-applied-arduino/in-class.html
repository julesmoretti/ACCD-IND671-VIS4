Today you design how your product talks to its user, and write it down in your HMI spreadsheet. Bring your Week 3 requirements list, your Week 4 test findings, your Week 7 hardware overview, and your breadboarded parts.

Exercise 1: Step Through the Hero Experience (15 min)

Write your hero experience as a sequence of steps, the same way you broke tasks into steps for testing. For each step, three columns:

Step The user does The device does / shows The screen shows
S1 Turns the dial to 40 Ring LEDs fill to match Target: 40

Exercise 2: States and Feedback (30 min)

  1. List every state your device can be in. Most products have at least: off, idle/ready, working, done, and error. Add disconnected: what happens when the WebSocket drops?
  2. For each state, decide how the user knows they are in it, on the device and on screen. Use more than one channel where it matters:
    • Visual: LED colour, blink pattern, display, screen
    • Auditory: tone, click, silence
    • Haptic: vibration, detent, resistance
  3. Check: can two states be confused? If "working" and "error" both blink, the user cannot tell them apart.
  4. Check your hardware overview: does every piece of feedback have a part that produces it? If not, add the part to your overview and your order list now.

Exercise 3: Run-Through in Pairs (25 min)

One person plays the device; the other is the user. The user tries the hero experience using your interface on screen. The "device" responds out loud and by hand: "the ring lights up to 40", "it beeps twice". No explaining.

Swap. Write down every moment where the user did not know what had happened. Those are missing or unclear feedback.

Exercise 4: Build Your HMI Spreadsheet (60 min)

You already have most of what goes in it. This exercise puts it in one place, in the format of the reading (Step 3).

  1. Set up your Google Sheet with the two tabs: Product Information and UX.
  2. In the UX tab, add one row for each requirement on your Week 3 requirements list. Keep its priority (Must-have, Should-have, Nice-to-have).
  3. Add one row for each interaction in your Week 7 hardware overview that is not on the list yet. The last column of the overview tells you what each row must say.
  4. Add the states and feedback from Exercise 2, and fix every gap Exercise 3 found.
  5. Group the rows into categories (for example Power, Setup, Main task, Errors).
  6. Finish at least the category that holds your hero experience. The rest is homework.

Your Week 4 test findings belong in the Notes column of the rows they affect.

Exercise 5: Plan the End-to-End Breadboard (10 min)

Look at your hero experience steps from Exercise 1. List the parts and messages you need to make every step happen on the breadboard, device to screen and screen to device. That list is your homework.
