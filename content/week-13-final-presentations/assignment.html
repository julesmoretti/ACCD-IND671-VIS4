You have presented. This week you turn the project into something that lasts: a finished HMI document, and a portfolio case study that someone who was not in the room can understand in two minutes.

Part 1: Act on the Presentation Feedback

Read the written feedback you were handed on Friday. Pick the piece that matters most and act on it: in the prototype, in the documentation, or in how you tell the story. Write one line on what you changed and why.

Part 2: Final HMI Document

Update your HMI Google Sheet with everything the three test rounds and the final presentation changed:

  • Every interaction in the final product has a row, with its feedback on the device and on screen.
  • Rows that changed because of testing say so in the Notes column, with the round and the finding.
  • Rows you designed but did not build are marked as such, with their priority.
  • The Product Information tab has a labelled diagram of the final prototype, not an earlier version.

Export it as a PDF.

Part 3: Finish the Object

If you did not finish the colours, materials and finish before the presentation, finish them now. The photos in Part 4 are only as good as the object in them.

Part 4: Portfolio Case Study

One page on your website (or a PDF of the same page if you do not have a site yet). Write it for a hiring manager who has never heard of this course. Suggested order:

  1. The product in one sentence, with a hero photo.
  2. Who it is for and the problem it solves.
  3. Where it started: the original studio object, and what it did not resolve.
  4. The hero experience: a short video of it working (30–60 seconds, no narration needed, captions are fine).
  5. How it works: the block diagram and a photo of the electronics, from breadboard to soldered.
  6. The interface: the device and the screen side by side, and how one reflects the other.
  7. What testing changed: the three-round comparison as one clear chart, and the finding that surprised you most.
  8. What you would do next.

Photography: plain background, soft light, one hero shot, one shot in a hand or in use, one close-up of the feedback (a light, a display, a detail). Make the video in one take where you can, from a cold start, the same way you demonstrated it.

Part 5: Tidy the Repo

Your project repo is part of the portfolio. Make sure the README says what the project is, shows a photo, lists the hardware, and explains how to start the demo. Remove files that are not part of the project.
