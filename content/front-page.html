Course Description

Visualization Communication 4 (Vis Com 4) is a one-semester (14-week) project-based course designed to bridge the gap between Industrial Design (ID) and Human-Machine Interface (HMI). The course focuses on creating a comprehensive experience by integrating hardware and software through bi-directional communication, culminating in a fully integrated hardware-software prototype validated through user testing. It is structured in three phases: software foundations, electronics and hardware, and integrated prototype development.

In the first phase, students build front-end fundamentals — HTML and CSS for interface structure and styling, JavaScript for interactivity, and React for component-based interfaces. These are applied to a software project presented at midterm.

The second phase introduces electronics fundamentals — soldering, voltage, measurement, breadboarding, and circuit documentation — followed by hardware prototyping with Arduino and WebSocket communication for real-time bi-directional interaction, alongside Human-Machine Interface (HMI) documentation concepts.

The final phase centers on an integrated hardware-software prototype. Students develop, test, and refine the prototype across three structured rounds of user testing, working across Arduino, React UI, and HMI documentation. Work throughout the term is individual and collaborative, with regular critique and feedback leading to a final presentation.

Prerequisites: This course is open to graduate students in Industrial Design or related disciplines.

Before Day One

Install Sublime Text, Node.js, Git, and Dash, plus Homebrew on Mac or Git Bash on Windows. Bring a laptop with admin privileges and these tools configured.

In Week 1 you will also identify a prior studio project to carry forward into this course, and order your common hardware kit. Component lead time is a real constraint here — parts ordered late will not arrive in time to build with, so ordering early is part of the work.

 

Program Learning Outcomes

The Program Learning Outcomes for the Master of Science in Industrial Design are designed to produce integrated abilities and levels of experience enabling graduates to confidently and effectively apply innovation methodologies to research and develop new value/solutions that balance the related humanistic, technological, and business dimensions for myriad complex unstructured problems (innovation contexts) including abilities to:

PLO 1: Systems Thinking
This course emphasizes end-to-end product integration, from hardware (Arduino) to software (React/JavaScript) and HMI documentation, aligning with the need to think in systems.

PLO 2: Human Centered Research
The focus on HMI documentation and interaction between users and devices ensures that students consider the human-centered aspects of design and user experience.

PLO 3: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving 
By introducing bi-directional communication with WebSockets between software and hardware and having students integrate hardware-software prototypes, this course fosters critical thinking and problem-solving skills.

PLO 4: Quality of Execution
Students are evaluated on the technical implementation of their projects, including clean code, functional prototypes, and well-documented processes. This speaks to the quality of execution expected in the program.

PLO 5: Strategic Thinking 
In applying the end-to-end learned processes to existing Industrial Design projects, students practice strategic thinking, integrating multiple facets of design, technology, and user interaction.

PLO 6: Professional Practice
The course grading criteria, which include communication skills and professional presentations, ensure students practice professional skills essential in the industry.

 

Student Work: LUMI Home Media Center

Milo Menghao Yuan designed and developed the LUMI Home Media Center in this course — a project that shows what a term here can produce. LUMI is a smart device that projects movies and information into space while doubling as a stylish floor lamp, adapting to various viewing preferences with adjustable angles (-45 to 90 degrees). Using the skills taught in this class — Arduino for hardware prototyping, React for UI, and WebSocket for real-time interaction — Milo turned the concept into a fully functional prototype. The device moves via mobile phone control, changes colors, and operates wirelessly without cords or power plugs. Learn more at designermilo.com/lumi.
